1/6 What if Lehman Brothers had run on Canton?

Not a claim that a blockchain could save it. I modeled authorization, collateral reuse and settlement in Daml, with 10 reproducible tests.

https://virajbhartiya.com/blog/what-if-lehman-brothers-had-run-on-canton

---

2/6 A repo with two signatories cannot be erased by either one alone. But BOTH can archive it without repayment.

Adding a supervisor as observer makes the contract visible to them. It doesn't enforce accounting treatment or guarantee a view of the whole firm.

---

3/6 The revealing test: cash transfer runs first, then the bond transfer fails authorization. Neither asset moves.

But the toy contract also accepts the WRONG payment asset if its quantity is sufficient. Atomicity enforces your rules, including incomplete ones.

---

4/6 Privacy needs deliberate design. A buyer can have enough cash and still fail to settle because the seller's bond wasn't disclosed.

The tests cover local Daml behavior. They don't prove network privacy or a global view across validators. Those claims need separate evidence.

---

5/6 The limits matter: custody, valuation, legal priority and liquidity risk remain. Locking collateral in advance can worsen a squeeze.

Disclosure: I work at SatsTerminal, building OneSwap on Canton. This article was not paid for. The toy-model tests are not a OneSwap audit.
